## Summary
### AI Contribution Guidelines Discussion
The team discussed updates from a chairs meeting regarding AI contributions to repositories. Pavan reported that the main focus was on creating a universal contributing guide that would list criteria for contributors, including requirements for attending work group meetings and demonstrating active collaboration. The group agreed to adopt this approach and create a contributing.md guide in their repository, with a focus on ensuring real human contributors rather than AI agents are involved in the process. Matt emphasized the importance of maintaining a community aspect to avoid having automated systems communicate with each other.

### AI Code Review Implementation
The group discussed implementing AI agents for initial code reviews to reduce review delays, with Joe suggesting the focus should be on error detection rather than content review. Pavan and Matt agreed to explore this as an action item, including potentially using existing interns from Joe's team. The conversation ended with Matt announcing plans to form a joint workstream across working groups focused on attestation, with Steven expressing interest in co-leading this initiative.

### Taxonomy Updates Working Group
The working group discussed taxonomy updates and identified volunteers to help define observability terms and create PRs against the repository. Sven, Matthew, Sal, Ariel, Dylan, and Govindaraj volunteered to contribute, with Joe planning to work directly with them to address process hurdles. The group discussed potential motivations and incentives for contributors, including gamification and recognition from companies, while Matt suggested explaining the end outcomes of the taxonomy work to help align corporate contributors. Pavan proposed that the taxonomy could follow a similar approach to CNCF's landscape publication, potentially becoming a standardized wiki of terms for the Agentic technology space.

### White Papers and Taxonomy Development
The group discussed developing white papers as meaningful deliverables from the AAIF to guide practitioners in the observability and traceability space. Joe suggested creating a spreadsheet to track specific observability and universal terms that need definitions or scope notes, which received positive feedback from the group. Pavan mentioned that contributors could suggest terms for the global taxonomy list, and Matt suggested linking to existing issues in the taxonomy working group to improve discoverability for potential contributors.

### Term Documentation Hierarchy Discussion
The team discussed challenges with term definition and documentation hierarchy, with Sal emphasizing the need to establish foundational terms before building higher layers to avoid extensive refactoring. The group agreed to move away from spreadsheet tracking and instead use GitHub issues for better discoverability and trackability, though Govindaraj raised concerns about permission requirements for issue assignment. Matt suggested bringing the hierarchy feedback to the taxonomy working workstream, and Pavan proposed tracking terms in the Observability repo before moving them to the taxonomy repo once ready.

### Taxonomy and Liaison Updates Discussion
The team discussed several topics including taxonomy terms, focus groups, and liaison updates. Pavan requested that someone submit undefined terms to next week's taxonomy call to provide context and examples. Govindaraj updated on focus group work with Steven and Imran regarding AIC integration, and Ty mentioned an updated AAC demo with attestation features. The conversation ended with Matt discussing the need to codify and commit a list of liaisons to the GitHub repository, asking for volunteers to help with liaison roles to other working groups or external standards bodies.

### Behavior Trace Model Collaboration Updates
The team discussed two main topics: cross-workstream collaboration and behavior trace model updates. Pavan mentioned Manik's email about potential collaboration with other working groups on compliance logging and identity attestation. Matt proposed reframing the behavior trace model approach to address semantic inconsistencies in OpenTelemetry implementations across different agents, suggesting the creation of a conformance framework within the AAF repository. The team agreed to identify high-impact areas for semantic convergence and to reach out to contacts at organizations like Microsoft, Google, Oracle, Red Hat, and AWS for potential liaison support.

### Agentic White Paper Planning
The team discussed structuring an agentic white paper, with Pavan sharing a document outlining potential content areas including agentic observability, debugging, reliability, and cost control. Matt raised concerns about the white paper becoming stale quickly due to the fast-moving nature of the AI space, leading to discussions about creating a living document with regular updates and interactive demos as suggested by Ty. Manu proposed submitting the work as an RFC at IETF conferences, particularly IETF 127 in San Francisco, and suggested combining both a constructionist approach with visual demos and a more formal instructional design approach through IETF submissions.
